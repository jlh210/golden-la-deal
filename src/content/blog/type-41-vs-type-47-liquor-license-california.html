Type 41 vs. Type 47 Liquor License: What's the Difference?

7 min readPublished August 27, 2026By Richard Stein, COO
Key Takeaways
  • Type 41 allows on-sale beer and wine only, at a bona fide eating place.
  • Type 47 allows on-sale general (beer, wine, and distilled spirits) at a bona fide eating place.
  • Both require the location to operate primarily as a restaurant, not a bar: the ABC calls this the 'bona fide eating place' requirement.
  • Type 47 licenses generally cost more and take longer to acquire or transfer than Type 41.
  • Some Type 47 locations carry a Conditional Use Beverage (CUB) overlay from the city that adds its own restrictions on top of the state license.

The short answer

A Type 41 license lets a restaurant serve beer and wine only. A Type 47 license lets a restaurant serve beer, wine, and full distilled spirits, a complete bar program. Both are 'on-sale' licenses meant for consumption on the premises, and both legally require the business to operate as a bona fide eating place, meaning food service is the primary function, not an afterthought to the bar.

Type 41: on-sale beer and wine, eating place

Type 41 is the right fit for concepts built around food where beer and wine round out the menu rather than drive the business: think casual dining, pizza, most fast-casual concepts, and neighborhood restaurants without a cocktail program. It's typically faster and less expensive to acquire than a Type 47, and in most parts of Los Angeles County it's easier to find available inventory for.

  • Covers: beer, wine, and sake for on-site consumption.
  • Does not cover: distilled spirits (vodka, whiskey, tequila, rum, gin, etc.) in any form, including in cocktails.
  • Requires: a genuine food-service operation, the ABC can and does verify this.

Type 47: on-sale general, eating place

Type 47 covers everything Type 41 does, plus full distilled spirits, meaning a complete cocktail program. It's the license most full-service restaurants with a bar area want, and it's generally the more expensive and harder-to-source of the two, particularly for a restaurant that wants an already-issued license rather than applying for a new one from the ABC directly.

In some Los Angeles neighborhoods, a Type 47 location also carries a Conditional Use Beverage permit (a CUB) issued by the city or county on top of the state ABC license. A CUB can layer on its own conditions: hours of operation, occupancy limits, noise mitigation, or a requirement that alcohol sales stay under a certain percentage of total sales. Buyers need to review the CUB conditions attached to a specific address, not just assume the state license tells the whole story.

Type 41 vs. Type 47, side by side

  • What's served: Type 41 is beer and wine. Type 47 is beer, wine, and spirits.
  • Typical concept fit: Type 41 is casual and fast-casual dining. Type 47 is full-service dining with a bar program.
  • Relative cost to acquire: Type 41 is generally lower-cost; Type 47 generally carries a higher premium in the open market.
  • Local overlay risk: Type 47 locations are more likely to carry a city or county CUB with additional operating conditions.
  • Eating-place requirement: applies to both, neither license permits operating primarily as a bar.

How to actually get one in Los Angeles

There are two paths: a person-to-person transfer of an existing license (buying one that's already issued, either standalone or as part of a restaurant acquisition), or a new application directly through the California Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control. Which path makes sense, and how long it takes, depends on current license availability for your license type in your specific area, and whether the address you're looking at already carries a CUB or other local entitlement. This is where a licensing specialist who works LA transactions regularly earns their fee: getting the wrong answer here can add months to a deal or kill it in escrow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered directly.

What's the difference between a Type 41 and Type 47 liquor license?+

Type 41 permits on-sale beer and wine only. Type 47 permits on-sale beer, wine, and full distilled spirits. Both require the business to operate primarily as a restaurant.

Can I upgrade from a Type 41 to a Type 47 later?+

You can't 'upgrade' a Type 41 into a Type 47. They're separate license types. You would need to acquire a Type 47 license (by transfer or new application) and hold it in place of, or alongside, your existing license, depending on your setup.

Do I need a Type 47 to serve cocktails?+

Yes. Any restaurant serving distilled spirits, including in mixed cocktails, needs a Type 47 (or another full-liquor license type). A Type 41 only covers beer and wine.

What is a 'bona fide eating place' requirement?+

It's the ABC's requirement that a business holding a Type 41 or Type 47 operates primarily as a restaurant, with a real kitchen, a real menu, and food as the core of the business, rather than functioning as a bar that happens to serve food.

What is a CUB and does it affect my license?+

A Conditional Use Beverage permit is a local land-use entitlement, separate from your state ABC license, that some cities and the county require for alcohol service at a given address. It can impose its own hours, occupancy, or operating conditions on top of what the state license allows, always check whether a specific location carries one before assuming the state license tells the whole story.
