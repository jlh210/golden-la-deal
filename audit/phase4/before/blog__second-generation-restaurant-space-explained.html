What Is a Second-Generation Restaurant Space? (And Why It Can Save You Six Figures)

6 min readPublished August 27, 2026By Richard Stein, COO
Key Takeaways
  • A second-generation space previously operated as a restaurant and still has kitchen infrastructure in place.
  • Existing hood, grease trap, and grease interceptor systems are the single biggest build-out cost a second-gen space saves you.
  • 'Second-gen' doesn't mean 'move-in ready': permits, equipment condition, and code compliance still need to be verified.
  • These spaces turn over constantly in LA as restaurants close or relocate: ask your broker for current inventory before touring raw shells.

The short answer

A second-generation restaurant space is a location that previously operated as a restaurant and still has some or all of the kitchen infrastructure in place, most importantly the exhaust hood, grease trap or interceptor, and often walk-in refrigeration and existing plumbing runs. Building that infrastructure from scratch in a raw retail shell (a 'first-generation' or 'vanilla box' space) is one of the largest line items in any restaurant build-out, which is why second-gen spaces are so valuable to buyers on a budget or a timeline.

Why it matters for your build-out cost

A commercial kitchen hood system with fire suppression, ductwork, and make-up air, plus a grease interceptor tied into the sewer line, routinely represents one of the largest single categories of a ground-up restaurant build-out, and that's before walk-in coolers, plumbing for a full kitchen, and the electrical service upgrade a commercial kitchen typically requires. A second-gen space that already has usable versions of this infrastructure can turn a project that would otherwise take months of permitting and construction into one focused mainly on cosmetic build-out and equipment.

What 'second-gen' does not mean

Second-generation is not a guarantee of move-in-ready condition. It means the infrastructure exists, not that it currently passes inspection, matches your concept's equipment layout, or is sized correctly for your intended volume. Treat every second-gen space as a starting point to verify, not a finished product to assume.

What to verify even in a second-gen space

  • Does the hood and fire suppression system currently pass inspection, or has it lapsed since the last tenant?
  • Is the grease trap or interceptor sized appropriately for your concept, and has it been maintained?
  • Are the electrical service and gas line capacity sufficient for your equipment plan, not just the prior tenant's?
  • What permits does the space carry, and do they transfer or require a new plan-check submittal for a change of use or ownership?
  • Is there any unpermitted work from a prior tenant that the city will flag once you file for your own permits?

Where to find one in LA

Second-gen inventory turns over constantly as restaurants close, relocate, or get acquired, but it rarely gets marketed as a distinct category on general commercial listing sites. A broker working LA restaurant deals specifically will usually know what's available off-market or about to hit the market before it's broadly listed, which matters because these spaces tend to move fast once they're identified.

Buying the business vs. taking over the space

Sometimes a second-gen space comes attached to an operating business for sale, and sometimes it's simply a vacant space with a prior restaurant's infrastructure left behind. The due diligence is different for each: an operating business sale requires the full financial and license review; a vacant second-gen space is more about verifying the physical infrastructure and permit status.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered directly.

What does 'second-generation restaurant space' mean?+

It refers to a commercial space that previously operated as a restaurant and retains some or all of the kitchen infrastructure (hood, grease trap, plumbing), reducing build-out cost and time for the next tenant.

Is a second-generation space always cheaper to build out?+

It's usually significantly cheaper than a raw shell, but not automatically move-in ready. Equipment condition, code compliance, and whether the layout fits your concept all still need to be verified and can still require real investment.

Do I need new permits if I take over a second-gen restaurant space?+

In most cases yes: a change of ownership or change of use typically triggers a new health permit review and often a plan-check submittal, even when the physical infrastructure stays largely the same.

How do I find second-generation restaurant spaces in Los Angeles?+

General commercial real estate listings rarely categorize spaces this way. A broker who works restaurant transactions specifically typically has visibility into current and upcoming second-gen inventory, including off-market opportunities.

Can I change the concept in a second-gen space, or does it have to match the prior restaurant?+

You can generally change concept, but the existing infrastructure was built for the prior tenant's needs: a heavy-grease concept's hood and grease interceptor may be oversized or undersized for a very different type of kitchen, so verify fit rather than assuming it.
